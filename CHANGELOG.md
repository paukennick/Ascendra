# Changelog

## Unreleased

- REQ-123: Hands-on labs you can be graded on. Besides the do-it-yourself labs some CompTIA courses already had, there are now build labs (you produce a real artifact, such as an IAM policy, a work breakdown structure, an SBAR handoff or a first training program) and case labs (a fictional situation that unfolds over two to four steps, each showing new information once you've answered the one before). Every part of your answer is scored as met, partly there or not yet, with the model answer beside yours and kind feedback, and a finished lab counts toward mastery on the objective it practises (a second try after seeing the answers counts lightly). Answers stay on your device until you send them. 58 pilot labs for AWS Solutions Architect Associate, NCLEX-RN, PMP, NASM-CPT and CCRN (Adult, plus one case each for Pediatric and Neonatal) are written and waiting for expert review; they appear once a subject expert approves them. Labs can be reported like any question. Migration 050 (see the deploy runbook; build its one index concurrently first on a large database). docs/labs.md explains grading, mastery, safety rules, authoring, how many labs each unit should get, and the generator for adding more.
- REQ-121: Reusing an answer's id on a different question is now refused the same way everywhere. A Quiz me answer sent with an id already used for another question no longer gets that question's result and feedback back; it is refused and nothing is recorded, while a resend of the same answer still gets its result. A lab report sent with an id already used on another lab now gets the same "already used for a different question" response as the other graded screens (the app shows it the same way). A unit check you already sent always gets its result back on a resend, even if the unit has since gained a lesson you haven't studied yet. No migration.
- REQ-120: Sending the same answer twice at once (a double tap, or a flaky connection resending it) now gets it graded once everywhere, not just on lesson answers, scenarios and reviews. Unit checks, explain-it-back and lab reports now share one grading among copies of one submission and give every copy the same result, so a resend no longer costs extra AI calls. Reusing an answer's id on a different question is still refused. The deploy runbook's 046 row now points at the right step (1a).
- REQ-117: Your levels, readiness and plan now tell the same, honest story. A scenario question (PBQ) or unit check still counts toward every objective in its unit, but as accuracy only and for less on a big unit, so one scenario no longer makes a whole unit Transfer-ready; answering the same scenario again after seeing its model answer counts as a repeat. Getting a question right with help (the choices on screen, or a question you've seen before) now counts for less weight instead of counting as partly wrong, so practice that goes well never lowers your level. Comparison cards count toward Transfer-ready but can't make an objective Independent on their own. Transfer-ready now needs a second format, such as explaining it back, at least three days after your first strong answer and a day or more after your last one. Independent needs 70% weighted accuracy (was 60%) and Transfer-ready 80% (was 75%). "Needs review" waits until you've been checked again on a later day, and a placement answer alone no longer makes an objective Guided. "Kept" now means you answered it right, without help, when its review was due (the latest and two of the last three). Readiness falls the longer you're away, an objective counts fully only once it's Independent and kept, "ready" needs 70% of the exam kept, and the course page shows how much is confirmed. After a long break an objective is marked due to re-check. Today's review: answering after showing the choices, typed or picked, counts as helped, which brings the question back within a week rather than every day; a miss at the 3- or 9-week step comes back in 3 days and rebuilds; after two misses in a row a question opens with a short refresher from the lesson and the choices. The plan counts due reviews the way the review screen does, confirms what your practice exams already show, and once you're through the course offers stretch steps (explain it back). Placement asks up to one question per three objectives (12 to 30) and skips what a later right answer covers. Explain-it-back spots text pasted from the lesson even with filler words added, sentences reordered or a few words swapped. A garbled reply from the grader no longer loses your answer, and a double-tapped submit is graded once. Grading stays fast for learners with tens of thousands of answers. Needs migrations 045 (under 2 seconds) and 045b (backs up mastery and recomputes it under the new rule; see docs/deploy-runbook-integration.md) before the backend deploys, then `call recompute_mastery_batched();`. Many levels change, most often Transfer-ready to Independent.
- REQ-114: Questions are now checked before anyone sees them, and you can report one that looks wrong. Before a new shared question (practice exam, Quiz me, Today's review, comparison cards, a lesson's checks) goes live, a second, cheaper AI answers it without seeing the answer key, and the question is published only if the two agree. Under every graded question there is now "Report a problem with this question". A question is taken out of rotation for everyone after three people report it, or after one report if an independent re-check also disagrees with its answer; if a person then confirms the answer was wrong, your answers to it stop counting against your mastery (they stay in your history). When the grader is confident a lesson's own answer key is wrong it now grades you on the facts, says so, and reports the question itself. Fixes: the AWS Advanced Networking pass mark is 750, not 700; DevOps Engineer no longer teaches the retired AWS OpsWorks, and the Security Specialty and ML Engineer courses note that CodeGuru Security has ended; the coach no longer refers to every learner as "he"; all multiple-choice questions follow the same item-writing rules; the six personal-trainer and strength-coach courses now ask you to accept a short safety note first; several labs were reworded for safety (revoke a leaked secret before deleting it, keep a vulnerable lab VM off your network, test SSH keys before restarting sshd, search hashes rather than uploading files, scan only networks you run, never run decoded malware) and one now asks about availability and RTO rather than RPO; and the CySA+ and Security+ V7 courses say when their translated exams retire. Needs migration 042 (new tables and one column, under a second) before the backend deploys; `npm run items -- list` shows pulled and held-back items.

- REQ-116: Account security fixes from the third audit. Changing your email address now asks you to confirm it's you (your password, a code from your authenticator app or a backup code, or for a Google-only account a fresh Google sign-in), and your current address is emailed straight away with a link that cancels the change and signs out every device. With two-factor already on, adding an authenticator also asks you to confirm it's you, and turning on two-factor, changing your password or signing out everywhere now ends other devices' access at once instead of up to 15 minutes later (this device stays signed in). Two-factor codes are limited per account, not just per sign-in attempt, with a friendly 15-minute pause. Five wrong passwords now pause sign-in only from that device's network, so someone else can't lock you out, and a failed sign-in looks the same whether or not the address has an account. If someone registered your address before you, signing up yourself (or signing in with Google) removes the password they chose. Deleting your account now also deletes its sign-in log, and sign-in records are kept for 90 days at most; the data export now includes your sign-in log and devices. Google-only accounts can turn two-factor off with an emailed code. Very large profile photos are refused before they are opened, malformed requests get a clear error instead of a server error, and a few smaller hardening fixes. Needs migration 044 before the backend deploys, and CRON_SECRET set in Vercel for the daily purge (docs/deploy-runbook-integration.md).

New lessons and Quiz-me questions are checked before they are shared (no empty or duplicate options, one reason per option, length limits, written in the course language) and regenerated once if they fail. A private course's title is limited to 120 characters and treated as a name, never an instruction. Long coach conversations now send at most about 8,000 tokens of history, trimmed in fixed steps so prompt caching keeps working, and each learner has a daily AI budget ($0.50 by default, `AI_DAILY_BUDGET_USD`) for coach chat and lesson regenerations, with a plain message when it is reached; grading is never blocked by it. When the AI gives an unusable reply, the app now gets "try again in a moment" (503) instead of a generic error. Needs migration 043 (one index on ai_calls; seconds). New manual workflow "Live evals" (`.github/workflows/live-evals.yml`) runs the grading, guard, generation and caching evals against the real model with a $5 hard stop.

- REQ-109: Practice exams. Each course now has a Practice exam screen with two modes. A timed sprint is 10 mixed questions from across the course at the exam's own pace. A mock exam has the real exam's length, spreads its questions across the exam's domains by their official weights, and runs on the exam's full time limit, with an untimed option for anyone who needs it; you can flag questions to come back to, and your answers are saved as you go, so you can leave and resume. Questions now come in the formats each exam actually uses: multiple choice, "choose two/three", putting steps in order (with Move up and Move down buttons, no dragging), and matching (pick from a list for each item), some with an exhibit such as a log excerpt, an access policy, a firewall rule table or a described network diagram; AWS courses that only use multiple choice and multiple response get only those. Everything is graded on the server, the answers are never sent to the app before you submit, and choices are shuffled per learner. After submitting you see your score (whole questions only, as the exams score them, with partly right answers shown separately), a breakdown by domain and by pace, and for a mock, how the score compares with your readiness estimate and how much a score from that many questions can tell you. A short "how sure were you" view compares your 1-5 confidence with how often you were right ("when you were sure, you were right 9 of 10") with gentle advice. Answered questions count toward mastery like any other answer; a question you've answered before counts for less, and a blank counts against the score but not against your mastery. Questions are written once per objective and shared by everyone on the course, so they cost little. Readiness no longer counts exam sections that have been retired. Needs migration 038 before the backend deploys (new tables only, under a second).

- REQ-100: Mastery now measures depth across every kind of question, not just typed answers. Each objective's level comes from a weighted accuracy over all your graded work on it -- the Guided step, multiple choice, free response, and the unit's scenario questions (PBQs) -- where harder kinds of question count for more, an answer given with the choices hint or after seeing the answer earns reduced credit, getting it right after a gap of a day or more counts for more than repeating it in the same sitting, getting back something you missed on an earlier day counts extra, and old answers slowly count for less. Transfer-ready now needs strong accuracy plus correct, unaided answers in two different formats (for example a free response and a PBQ) on separate days; Independent needs good accuracy and a correct free response in your own words. Every weight and threshold is in one table so they can be tuned. Course readiness is now real: it rolls those per-objective scores up by the exam's official domain weights and shows how much of the course you've practiced separately from how accurate you are, so trying a few objectives perfectly reads as "Not enough evidence yet", not "ready"; the course page and mastery matrix say this in plain words with a per-domain breakdown. The coach no longer gives away the answer to a question you haven't answered yet (it gives a hint or asks a guiding question instead, and the server checks its reply), treats what you type as your message rather than as instructions, stays on the course, and frames mistakes as information. Answers count as helped the same way they do since REQ-097 even when an older app version recorded them as unaided (a repeated question, the choices hint, or the Solo step answered from the choices), and an answer revised after seeing feedback is left out of the score entirely. Levels nobody earned with a graded answer go back to Not started. Migrations 031 (schema, under a second) and 031b (saves everyone's current mastery to a backup table, then recomputes it under the new model, without blocking reads; about 90 seconds on a 1.9-million-answer test database) run before the backend deploys; after the deploy, run `select recompute_mastery()` once. Some levels change (most often Transfer-ready becomes Independent until a second format is done); the 031b header has the restore SQL, which leaves alone anything that changed after the backup. Backups are deleted with the account.

- REQ-098: Hardening from the second audit.
